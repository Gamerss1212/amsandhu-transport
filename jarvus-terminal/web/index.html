Press Run bots once. Every five minutes the bots scan every liquid crypto market and the most active US stocks (in market hours), run all 95 strategies on each, backtest any setup on that market's last 1,500 hours before touching it, let the Brain's 120 agents vote on it, and buy only what passes. Every fifteen seconds they check each open trade and sell at its stop, its target or its time limit. They keep running across restarts until you press Stop. Practice money: live prices, real fees, real slippage, no real funds.

Measured before release, walk-forward on 15 markets: the bots' rule made +0.22R per trade over 675 trades (about $3.70 on a trade risking $16.67) and beat random buying in all three test windows, but mostly on volatile small coins; on BTC/ETH-type majors it made +0.07R. One period, no guarantee. This screen is where you find out whether it holds.

The Brain

R

Every candidate trade is read by 120+ agents at once: one for each strategy, and 25 that watch what strategies do not (volatility, trend on three horizons, fees, time of day, gaps, VWAP, crowding, the backtest's measured edge, and what Bitcoin or the S&P 500 is doing). A learned coordinator weighs them and predicts the trade's result in R, after fees. It was trained on history and judged on the part of history it never saw, below. It keeps learning from every trade the bots close.

How to read this

Heat blends how fast a market already moves with whether it is speeding up or winding tight. High heat means a big move is likely. The direction is a coin flip.
Agree counts how many independent strategy families fired at once. Twelve trend strategies saying the same thing is one opinion, so they are grouped first.
Cost is the round trip as a share of your risk. Above 33% the trade is refused: fees would eat it before the idea gets a chance.
BUY means the structural boxes are ticked and the cost gate passed. It is not a forecast. Every plan carries a stop that defines the loss in advance.

The swarm

Every loaded strategy is run against every scanned market on each scan. Votes are grouped into families first, because a dozen strategies that all say "price is above a rising average" are one idea and not twelve, and agreement across different families is the only kind worth counting.

Research

Backtests every strategy against every market over deep history, splits the period in two, and judges each strategy only on the half it was not built on. The weights the swarm uses come from here. Ranking is by excess over a control that buys with no analysis at all, because in a rising market the control makes money too, and anything that cannot beat it is measuring the weather.
