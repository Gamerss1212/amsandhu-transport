# Round 2 results (2026-10-07): three new families, run once each

Pre-registration: `round2-preregistration.md` (committed with this report; written before the
runs). 6 runs, 12 trials, all in `trials.md`. Vectorized engine, 0.15% one way.
Buy-and-hold figures come from the grid report on the same data files.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8 ETFs | buy-and-hold (benchmark) | 0.60 | 6.2% | 33.7% | | |
| 8 ETFs | hold_brake_200_252 | 0.52 | 3.9% | 15.1% | t 2.43 | FAIL |

All six passed the A39 red team (no look-ahead found) and stayed positive at 2x costs.

**In plain words**
- **No edge proven.** None of the three passes every check.
- **Closest calls:**
  - `hold_brake` on crypto beat holding on return, Sharpe and drawdown. But its t-statistic (2.97)
    is just under 3, its PBO is 0.92, and the grid's noise panels made best-of-grid Sharpes of
    1.12 (median) on crypto from pure luck. So 1.03 is inside the luck range.
  - `inv_vol` on the 8 ETFs had a higher Sharpe than holding (0.69 vs 0.60), with a smaller
    drawdown, and passed the t-test (3.10). It fails PBO. With only two near-twin variants, PBO
    is close to a coin flip (the same open question as Phase 2). It also earned less than holding.
- **Drawdown brake (`dd_brake`)** did worst: it sells after falls and buys back later, which
  costs return.
- **What would count as real evidence now:** not more tuning. Paper-trade days, or a fresh
  dataset these rules have never seen. These rules are not wired into the paper account.
