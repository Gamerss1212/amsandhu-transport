# Research grid: 2026-10-06

Generated by `quantagents research`. Pre-registered in `docs/research/grid-preregistration.md`.

- Backtests run: **7,668**. That is 348 real-data variants at 1x costs, the same at 2x costs, 20 noise panels per universe, and the finalists' event-driven and time-shift runs.
- Costs: 0.15% one way. Fills at the next open. Holdings drift with prices.
- Trials charged to the DSR: 348 (every real-data variant on every universe).
- Data caveats: survivorship-biased: upper bound only; not point-in-time (history may be restated).

## Summary in plain words (written after the run)

**All four finalists FAIL the pre-registered rule.** Across 7,668 backtests, no strategy was
shown to beat simply holding the same funds.

- **Against cash, yes. Against buy-and-hold, no.**
  - In every universe, at least one variant beats holding cash beyond luck (SPA p ≤ 0.022).
    That only says long-only funds earned the normal market return.
  - Against buy-and-hold of the same funds, no grid gets close (SPA p 0.38 to 1.00).
  - **0 of 344** variants survive the false-discovery check.
- **Picking the winner does not work going forward.** Choose the best variant on 3 years and
  trade it the next year, and you do *worse* than buy-and-hold in every universe:

  | Universe | Out-of-sample Sharpe | Buy-and-hold |
  |---|---|---|
  | multi_asset | 0.51 | 0.87 |
  | sectors | 0.83 | 0.99 |
  | us_index | 0.61 | 0.94 |
  | crypto | 0.25 | 0.43 |

- **Luck makes good-looking winners.** On noise panels where no trend exists, the best of 87
  variants still reached a median Sharpe of 0.59 to 1.12, about as high as the real winners
  (0.68 to 1.22).
- **Why each finalist failed:**
  - **DSR:** 0.60 to 0.73 after 348 trials (needs 0.95).
  - **PBO:** 0.36 to 0.84 (needs ≤ 0.20).
  - **Walk-forward ratio** too low on multi_asset and crypto.
  - **No edge over buy-and-hold** (SPA and FDR) anywhere.
  - Two finalists did clear the noise control. The A39 red team and the event-driven re-test
    passed for all four.

### What the grid does show (exploratory: seen after the run, not evidence)

| us_index | 0.58, +10.0%, 54.6% | 74 of 86 | 0 of 86 |
| crypto | 0.72, +29.2%, 76.9% | 74 of 86 | 36 of 86 |

- **Trend and volatility rules cut losses.** Faber (15 of 16 runs), the trend blend (43 of 48)
  and volatility targeting (34 of 40) had a slightly higher Sharpe than buy-and-hold in almost
  every universe, by a median of +0.04 to +0.06. Their worst drops were much smaller.
  Outside crypto they earned less.
- **That edge is too small to prove.** A Sharpe difference of 0.05 is well inside the
  sampling error of a 10-25 year history.
- **RSI(2) was the worst family everywhere** (0 of 48 runs beat buy-and-hold).
- **A hypothesis worth a future, separately pre-registered test:** "Trend and volatility
  rules cut the worst losses at the same or better risk-adjusted return." It needs fresh data:
  the 30-day paper run, other markets, or years not used here.

### Supplementary check (after the run; does not change any verdict)

- The pre-registered event-driven re-test used the config's 10,000 CAD and whole units. On
  crypto it still bought BTC 129 times, because the account grew, and gave Sharpe 1.28 against
  1.22 vectorized.
- Re-run with fractional units (0.0001), it gives exactly 1.22. The check's PASS is sound.
